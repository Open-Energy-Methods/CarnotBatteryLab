# M3.9 — pressure-connected variable-property air bed and water heat exchanger

## Scope and benchmark distinction

M3.8's terminal-temperature controller passes the original 1 MW, 110→150°C water service on its *constant-property* industrial-size model: see the amended M3.8 report for exact numerical results. M3.9 instead extends the small-scale real-air mass/internal-energy prototype. It is not a replacement for that industrial model, does not change the original benchmark, and does not assume any verified source heat pump, real blower, installed HX, or 1 MW delivery.

The new model is an **open** pressure-reservoir train, with a separately prescribed water stream: upstream cool gas reservoir → assumed initially hot packed bed → pressure-connected counterflow gas/wall/water exchanger → downstream gas reservoir. No intermediate gas temperature, mass flow or pressure is prescribed at the bed/HX interface. The bed solid is explicitly initialized at 220°C; its initial energy is not generated by a simulated charge. No efficiency, charging credit or repeated cycle is inferred.

Bed wall loss and HX environmental loss are omitted in this prototype.

## Governing equations and numerical interface

For gas cell i, fixed pore or HX volume V_i, the states are extensive mass M_i and internal energy U_i. CoolProp HEOS Air obtains T,P,h,rho,mu and sound speed from rho=M_i/V_i and u=U_i/M_i.

    dM_i/dt = F_left - F_right
    dU_i/dt = F_left*h_upwind,left - F_right*h_upwind,right - Q_i

The solid bed inventory receives Q_gs = UA_gs (T_g - T_s). The HX gas transfers Q_gw = 2UA/N (T_g - T_wall) to the metal, and the wall transfers Q_wl = 2UA/N (T_wall - T_water) to the water. Opposite water advection is maintained in the spatial ordering; water specific enthalpy is an integrated state:

    M_water,i * dh_i/dt = mdot_water * (h_upwind - h_i) + Q_wl,i
    C_wall,i * dT_wall,i/dt = Q_gw,i - Q_wl,i

Water temperature is recovered by inverting the M3.6 single-phase enthalpy table at the assumed fixed pressure. Using enthalpy as the state avoids the previous numerical CP-versus-interpolated-enthalpy derivative discrepancy. This is an isobaric enthalpy-inventory approximation, not compressible water continuity.

Gas face fluxes are signed upwind, calculated from pressure-driven quasi-steady Ergun (bed) or Darcy/minor loss (HX) using upstream real-air density and viscosity where applicable. The interface includes the bed half-cell resistance plus the HX half-cell resistance in **series**; both use exactly the same mass flux. In mass-flow form the absolute pressure difference at the interface is c1|F|+c2|F|²; c1 comes from the Ergun viscous term and c2 includes Ergun inertial and HX Darcy terms. A numerically stable quadratic inversion supplies the shared flux. A finite Mach guard at accepted solver nodes rejects cases beyond the low-Mach domain. Gas kinetic energy, acoustics, axial conduction and distributed compressible pressure profiles within faces are omitted.

A stiff BDF integrator evolves both components in one vector. It also integrates left, right and shared-interface signed mass and enthalpy plus water boundary heating. The independently reconstructed inventories close:

    bed: ΔM_bed = M_left - M_interface
         ΔU_gas,bed + ΔU_solid = H_left - H_interface

    HX:  ΔM_HX = M_interface - M_right
         ΔU_gas,HX + ΔH_water + ΔU_wall = H_interface - H_right - Q_water

Interface terms cancel in the combined ledger. This is numerical verification of the stated reduced-order equations, not an experimental test. Low-Mach Darcy-only resistance assumes turbulent-like losses, uses a constant friction factor and cannot certify low-Re flow or hydraulic control across the whole operating range.

## Reproducible study and acceptance

Run seven predeclared 60 s cases: meshes 2/2, 4/4 and 8/8; increased HX minor loss K=40; reduced reservoir head from 200 to 100 Pa; initial cold solid at 110°C; and reduced HX UA from 120 to 30 W/K. These are model-only sensitivity controls, not parameter fitting. In particular, changed HX resistance must feed back into bed mass flux. All mass and energy ledgers are tested individually, not merely their sum. The cold-bed control checks that the model does not spontaneously produce meaningful water heat, while allowing small real-gas throttling transients. The pressure-phase rejection uses an assumed water pressure below the 150°C liquid requirement.

    python -m pip install -e ".[physics,test]"
    python -m pytest tests/test_m3_9_open_real_air_water_train.py tests/test_m3_8_real_air_bed_prototype.py tests/test_m3_8_terminal_and_real_air.py -q
    python scripts/run_m3_9_open_air_water_train.py

The dedicated workflow archives results/m3_9_open_air_water_train.json. Inspect the actual numerical grid sensitivity and separate energy/mass residuals before using any results. No qualification or RTE may be promoted.

## Verified numerical results

[Initial dedicated CI run 44406525734](https://github.com/Open-Energy-Methods/CarnotBatteryLab/actions/runs/44406525734) passed 21 M3.8/M3.9 tests and all seven declared 60 s cases. These are results for the small open pressure-driven prototype, not the industrial contract.

| Case | Final water °C | Mean water thermal duty kW | Integrated shared-interface gas mass kg | Final gas exit kg/s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2/2 cells | 136.398 | 1.367 | 2.6002 | 0.04338 |
| 4/4 cells | 143.224 | 1.802 | 2.5676 | 0.04285 |
| 8/8 cells | 147.393 | 2.097 | 2.5499 | 0.04257 |
| HX minor-loss K=40, 4/4 | 139.412 | 1.599 | 1.9957 | 0.03321 |
| 100 Pa reservoir head, 4/4 | 137.071 | 1.474 | 1.7315 | 0.02887 |
| Cold solid, 4/4 | 110.000 | -0.00000863 | 2.8320 | 0.04720 |
| HX UA=30 W/K, 4/4 | 126.965 | 0.752 | 2.5634 | 0.04279 |

The hypothetical change in HX minor-loss coefficient 2→40 reduces the integrated gas mass at the shared bed-HX interface by 22.27%, and mean water duty by 11.27%. Lowering reservoir pressure head from 200 to 100 Pa reduces the same integrated mass by 32.56%. Because the initial gas inventories can change, interface and boundary flows need not be equal instantaneously. The cold-solid case has negligible signed water heat rather than a positive invented useful output.

Across the seven cases the largest absolute combined mass residual is ~1.34e-15 kg and combined energy residual ~1.50e-8 J. Both component ledgers were also checked. Maximum accepted-node interstitial/HX Mach is ~0.0524. These residuals verify conservation of the reduced equations, not fidelity of physical parameters.

Final water-temperature shifts of 6.826 K (2/2→4/4) and 4.169 K (4/4→8/8) are large. Mean water heat also shifts materially. Joint mesh refinement is not an independent convergence study of each component, and no accepted error bound or extrapolated design output is asserted. None of the baseline mesh cases reaches a final water temperature of 150°C in the 60 s window, and the prescribed 0.02 kg/s water stream is deliberately not the original 1 MW service. Do not scale these results to an industrial unit or infer its efficiency.

## Remaining gates

A closed pressure-connected air return loop needs a mass-conserving blower pressure/flow/enthalpy relation, real pressure inventory, gas cooler with explicit secondary sink, realistic valve and branch flow, startup charge, and a validated variable-Re heat-transfer closure. The current flow train cannot meet the original industrial benchmark by mere scaling. Original Weber measured sensor files, independent calibration and whole-run holdout validation remain unavailable; compressor maps and industrial water HX hardware are separate blockers. No industrial 1 MW service, experimental performance or electrical Carnot RTE is established here.
