# M3.18 — preliminary transcritical high-temperature heat pump screen

## Primary evidence / citation provenance
The papers were supplied directly by OST team leader Leon Brendel, who referred questions to Tim Pfeiffer.

1. T. Pfeiffer et al. (2026), *Transcritical high-temperature heat pump with hydrocarbon mixtures – experimental setup and model predictions*, 15th IEA HPC 2026, user-supplied PDF pp.1-7. The prototype is about 20 kW, BITZER 2DME-7K compressor, 9.2 m³/h at 50 Hz, nominal compressor max discharge 160°C; published modelling example heat-source water 90→60°C, heat sink 100→150°C; predicted R290/R601a (60/40 mass fraction) COP ~2.85 vs R600a ~2.58. **These are predictions, not measured mixture performance.**
2. T. Pfeiffer et al. (2026), *Transcritical High-Temperature Heat Pump with Hydrocarbon Mixtures: Experimental Results*, Purdue IRACC paper 2177, user-supplied PDF pp.1,4-7. Measured source 100→70°C, sink 100→150°C, compressor 60 Hz. Twelve steady-state points; only selected optimum values tabulated: R600a Q=12.9±0.34 kW, COP=3.01±0.29, discharge=153.6±1.9°C, pdis=4399±198 kPa; R290/R601a 50/50 Q=17.3±0.4 kW, COP=3.39±0.28, discharge=162.4±1.9°C, pdis=4725±200 kPa. Other conditions are not individually published. The mixture's measured discharge exceeds the 160°C nameplate figure given in the first paper; query the applicable rating/measurement before hardware qualification. No performance claim for n-butane R600 or steam R718 follows from isobutane R600a.

## Exact scientific boundary
This new module is a *steady-state thermodynamic candidate screen*, NOT coupled HP–TES simulation or measured compressor map. CoolProp HEOS thermodynamics are used, not the paper's REFPROP mixture implementation or its GERG mixture parameters. Mass fractions are converted to mole fractions for CoolProp mixture strings. EOS failures are explicit evidence gaps and are not replaced by fake pure-fluid maps. No pressure drop, receiver, flash separation, intermediate supercritical valve, internal heat exchanger, ejector, composition drift, flammability/HazOp design, compressor volumetric curve or validated speed dependence. Compressor isentropic efficiency is assumed 65%, motor efficiency 95%, volumetric efficiency 75%, heat-source and sink water both assumed 2 MPa. Condenser/gas-cooler profile and evaporator profile are screened at 11 counterflow points each against an assumed >=5 K pinch, with specified water source/sink inlet/outlet. Pressure grid of 4 low-side dew-temperature levels and 8 high-side critical-pressure multipliers 1.02–1.5 is a discrete exploratory sample, not continuous optimization. Compressor pressure limited to 8 MPa, discharge temperature to nominal 160°C.

Compute suction T,P, entropy, compressor isentropic end h, high-pressure actual h, water gas-cooler h(T,P), isenthalpic expansion, source heat, sink heat, compressor shaft and motor electricity, 1 MW reference refrigerant flow, required heat-source water flow, and volumetric flow at suction. Explicit first law Qsink = Qsource + Wshaft (not total electrical input; motor loss is to environment). COP includes assumed compressor motor electricity but excludes pumps/heat exchangers, and cannot be compared quantitatively as validated against the Purdue electrical measurements because fitted compressor physics and uncertainties are missing. Report grid rejections rather than silently discarding out-of-envelope runs.

## CRITICAL interface gate
CarnotBatteryLab's dynamically simulated gas heater targets 463.15 K (190°C) during charging. A 150°C output water stream can deliver 150°C process heat on DISCHARGE, but cannot charge gas to 190°C. At a hypothetical 5 K approach, the HP sink must deliver >=195°C water to the charging gas interface, AND actual heat transfer rate/duty and the HP compressor hardware need to be feasible. A 195°C liquid sink output is an exploratory *alternative*, not evidence that OST's 150°C experiments demonstrated it. The 160°C nominal compressor discharge rating cannot drive a 195°C sink at 5 K pinch in this prototype. A booster or different compressor/cycle/storage interface is necessary; no valid 1 MW Carnot charging architecture is established by the present screen.

The original source 80°C is separately simulated as an assumed 80→60°C source (the 60°C outlet is a new assumption), contrasted with OST's measured 100→70°C. Sink 110→150°C is hypothetical process heating and not equivalent to storage charging. Scenarios 110→195°C represent the gas-heater target +5K boundary and should be rejected when discharge-temperature rating/pinch is violated.

## Verification and release gate
Nine cases in matrix, plus unit and negative tests and full provenance. Aggregate only all nine case artifacts; compare source conditions and temperature compatibility. A valid pure- or mixture-fluid state-point screen is not compressor hardware validation; a no-feasible-result is a meaningful negative outcome. Do not fit compressor efficiency to the three published optimum points. Request from Tim Pfeiffer all 12 measured points with suction/discharge P,T, mass flow, power, speed, composition, temperatures, uncertainty and sharing terms. Hold R600, R718 and mixture hardware qualification and matched-service TES battery claims explicitly unqualified until supported.
