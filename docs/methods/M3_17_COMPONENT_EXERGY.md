## Reference, boundaries and units
Specify fixed environmental temperature T0=298.15 K (not an exergy validation). For fixed-volume air cells and solids/walls, changes in nonflow physical exergy use delta U-T0 delta S, and for modeled constant-pressure water inventory delta H-T0 delta S. Gas entropy comes independently from CoolProp HEOS::Air DmassUmass updates at each actual gas cell mass/energy/volume state. Water entropy is CoolProp HEOS::Water at the same 2 MPa and temperature table as its existing real enthalpy; linear interpolation is retained and not claimed exact EOS differentiation. For constant-heat-capacity rock and walls, entropy inventory is C ln(T/T0), and optional buffer similarly. No chemical exergy, kinetic or elevation energy, variable gas chamber volume, unknown material entropy coefficients or external heat-pump compressor performance.

Process water at fixed delivery pressure: integrate total delivered mass flow times (s_mixed-s_in) directly as an ODE ledger; useful exergy export is Qwater-T0*Sproduct. HX-branch entropy separately integrates mx(sHX-sin), with a distinct ideal bypass mixing-generation ledger (Sproduct-SHX); do not credit mixing as new heat. Signed cooler exergy export to the assumed infinite 100 C thermal sink is Qcool*(1-T0/Tsink), and environmental heat exergy is Q*(1-T0/Tboundary) for assumed ambient boundaries. Control-volume entropy balance also accounts for electrical heater and motor heat losses as ambient heat at T0; electric work supplies no entropy.

For every charge and discharge stage, form an independently property-based endpoint entropy inventory change. Compute total modeled entropy production Sgen = deltaS_inventory + S_product + Qcool/Tsink + sum(Qambient/Tambient) + (Eheater-Qheater+Eblower-Wblower)/T0. Compare electrical exergy in minus useful water export, cooler export, ambient exergy export, and stored exergy change with T0*Sgen. These two expressions are linked by the established first-law conservation check, so their agreement is a consistency test, **not independent experimental validation of second-law physical fidelity**.

## Component attribution and missing terms
Explicitly report strictly identifiable model contributions: electric resistance heater exergy conversion Eheat-(1-T0/Treturn)*Qheat, gas/rock heat-transfer entropy production Qgs(1/Trock-1/Tgas), gas/HX-wall and wall/liquid heat-transfer entropy generation, cooler gas-to-sink heat entropy generation, bypass adiabatic mixing entropy, ambient heat-transfer entropy generation for rock/wall/gas/buffer, and motor efficiency heat loss Eblower-Wblower. All are integrated from the solver ODE and use actual cell temperatures at each step. The remainder between total exergy destruction and sum of named terms is **unresolved gas hydraulic, finite-volume numerical and fluid-mixing exergy**; it is not silently assigned to the blower or bed. A negative remainder flags thermodynamic inconsistency/surrogate model limitations; do not force nonnegative components, impute missing terms or claim complete entropy generation. Pressure-loss and blower hardware maps and independent measurements remain missing.

Process-water and coolant pumping are in the external industrial electricity summary but not the modeled control-volume states; pump electricity is reported separately and not fabricated as a component of the closed-loop exergy balance. Exergy ratio using full plant electrical input can be computed for eligible service cases but is NOT Carnot round-trip efficiency or a qualified industrial efficiency. The original 80 C waste-source heat pump has yet to be implemented in the closed-bed model.

## Scope of execution
Eight 10-cycle cases, 12/16 mesh and 2.5/5 s time sensitivity for 500 W/K loss, plus adiabatic 3/5 MW heaters, 100 and 1000 W/K stress tests, and 1 MW forced cooler. Verify mass, first law, ledger/exergy closure, actual service, thermal limits and periodicity separately; do not relax failed service gates to obtain a favorable exergy result. Report loss-case sensitivity; grids may not establish asymptotic convergence.
